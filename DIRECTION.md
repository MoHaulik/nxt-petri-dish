# Petri Dish: direction (short)

The full version, with sources, is `docs/RESEARCH_AND_DIRECTION.md`.

- **Look:** a 1950s American teaching lab, drawn straight: honey agar, seafoam and cream enamel, oxblood Bakelite, a black crinkle microscope, one warm lamp. The life in the dish is drawn like the Bell System Science Series films (Capra, 1956 to 1957): real lab, mid-century cartoon life.
- **The arc:** sparks, cells, colonies, structures, beings, then citizens you can see with your own eyes. Their town grows into atomic-age architecture, and they build a monument of your hand.
- **Hands:** sticky pick, pinch to squeeze. Pinch near a dropper and it stays in your hand; every pinch after that is one drop. Put it back by bringing it to the rack.
- **Puzzle:** the dish flashes colour, glyph and note. Answer in order. Wrong colour kills the dish (comically) before life exists, and blights a corner of town after.
- **Microscope:** swing it over the dish, lean into the eyepiece, see the same simulation at 40x in bright field.
- **Voice:** mic loudness, onsets and pitch, no words. Loud = cheer, hum = they hum along, quiet = hush, clap = startle. Optional, asked for on the landing page.
- **Tech:** die Hände's single-file three r150.1 skeleton. Plane detection, hit-test and anchors as optional extras. No depth sensing, no post-processing, no `transmission`.
- **Anti-slop:** no sci-fi lab, no mad-scientist parody, no cute mascots, no glow on everything, no AI images, no stock HDRIs, no text walls.
